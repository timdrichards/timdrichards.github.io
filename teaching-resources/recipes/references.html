The research behind the recipes

Every source the recipes cite, in a sentence or two, with the recipes that use it. Most of it comes from education research; some from computing education. Together they show how much of what works in a CS course is already well studied.

  1. Ahmed, Sahai, Leong and Karkare (2025). Feasibility Study of Augmenting Teaching Assistants with AI for CS1 Programming Feedback. SIGCSE Technical Symposium. doi:10.1145/3641554.3701972

    TAs at IIT Kanpur gave CS1 feedback with AI drafts. Students rated the feedback higher, but scores did not improve, and some TAs passed on the AI's errors without checking.

    Used in: 2. Faster feedback with AI drafts and a person in the loop, 32. When AI drafts the grading, what TAs do instead Spring 2028

  2. Atkinson, Derry, Renkl and Wortham (2000). Learning from Examples: Instructional Principles from the Worked Examples Research. Review of Educational Research. doi:10.3102/00346543070002181

    A review of worked-example research. For novices, studying worked examples teaches more than solving problems cold, and examples work best when steps are faded out over time.

    Used in: 6. Worked examples, then practice Spring 2027

  3. Bearman, Tai, Dawson, Boud and Ajjawi (2024). Developing evaluative judgement for a time of generative artificial intelligence. Assessment & Evaluation in Higher Education. doi:10.1080/02602938.2024.2335321

    Argues that assessment should build students' evaluative judgement, the ability to tell good work from poor, for AI output and AI processes as well as for their own work.

    Used in: 2. Faster feedback with AI drafts and a person in the loop, 27. Students giving themselves feedback Fall 2027

  4. Biggs (1996). Enhancing teaching through constructive alignment. Higher Education. doi:10.1007/BF00138871

    The origin of constructive alignment. Learning outcomes, teaching activities and assessment should all point at the same thing, so that doing the assessed work is doing the learning.

    Used in: 8. Assignments and exams that measure the outcomes Spring 2027, 28. Revising your course from the evidence Fall 2027, 42. Handing the course to next term Spring 2028, 43. Exams students can prepare for, 44. A syllabus that runs the semester for you

  5. Black and Wiliam (1998). Assessment and Classroom Learning. Assessment in Education: Principles, Policy & Practice. doi:10.1080/0969595980050102

    The landmark review of formative assessment. Checks that teachers and students act on while learning is still under way produce some of the largest gains reported in education research.

    Used in: 2. Faster feedback with AI drafts and a person in the loop, 11. Formative assessment: acting on results while there is time Spring 2027, 43. Exams students can prepare for

  6. Blunt and Karpicke (2014). Learning with retrieval-based concept mapping. Journal of Educational Psychology. doi:10.1037/a0035934

    Students who drew a concept map from memory, then checked it, learned about as much as students who took practice tests. What mattered was retrieval, not the map.

    Used in: 43. Exams students can prepare for

  7. Carless and Boud (2018). The development of student feedback literacy: enabling uptake of feedback. Assessment & Evaluation in Higher Education. doi:10.1080/02602938.2018.1463354

    Feedback only helps when students can understand it, judge it and act on it. The paper sets out what that "feedback literacy" is and how teachers can build it.

    Used in: 26. TAs, feedback and AI Fall 2027, 32. When AI drafts the grading, what TAs do instead Spring 2028

  8. Cepeda, Pashler, Vul, Wixted and Rohrer (2006). Distributed practice in verbal recall tasks: A review and quantitative synthesis. Psychological Bulletin. doi:10.1037/0033-2909.132.3.354

    A meta-analysis of the spacing effect. Practice spread over time is remembered much longer than the same practice massed together.

    Used in: 3. Low-stakes quizzes that teach Spring 2027, 43. Exams students can prepare for

  9. Chi, Bassok, Lewis, Reimann and Glaser (1989). Self-Explanations: How Students Study and Use Examples in Learning to Solve Problems. Cognitive Science. doi:10.1207/s15516709cog1302_1

    Students who explained worked examples to themselves learned more than those who did not. The source of the self-explanation effect.

    Used in: 20. Checking understanding by explaining back Fall 2027

  10. Cohen (1980). Effectiveness of student-rating feedback for improving college instruction: A meta-analysis of findings. Research in Higher Education. doi:10.1007/BF00976252

    Instructors who received midterm feedback from students got better end-of-term ratings than those who did not, especially when the feedback came with consultation.

    Used in: 40. Midsemester feedback from students and staff Spring 2028

  11. Crouch and Mazur (2001). Peer Instruction: Ten years of experience and results. American Journal of Physics. doi:10.1119/1.1374249

    Ten years of peer instruction at Harvard. Students answer a concept question, discuss it with a neighbor and answer again, and conceptual understanding rose year over year.

    Used in: 19. Peer instruction Fall 2027

  12. Dawson, Bearman, Dollinger and Boud (2024). Validity matters more than cheating. Assessment & Evaluation in Higher Education. doi:10.1080/02602938.2024.2386662

    With generative AI everywhere, the real question about an assessment is whether it gives valid evidence of what a student can do, not whether it catches cheating.

    Used in: 22. GitHub history that shows each student's own work Fall 2027

  13. Dickson and Miller (2005). Authorized Crib Cards Do Not Improve Exam Performance. Teaching of Psychology. doi:10.1207/s15328023top3204_6

    Letting students bring a crib card to the exam did not improve their scores.

    Used in: 43. Exams students can prepare for

  14. Ericson, Margulieux and Rick (2017). Solving Parsons problems versus fixing and writing code. Koli Calling International Conference on Computing Education Research. doi:10.1145/3141880.3141895

    Students who solved Parsons problems (putting given lines of code in order) learned as much as students who fixed or wrote the same code, and finished in less time.

    Used in: 21. Parsons problems and live coding Fall 2027

  15. Freeman, Eddy, McDonough, Smith, Okoroafor, Jordt and Wenderoth (2014). Active learning increases student performance in science, engineering, and mathematics. Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences. doi:10.1073/pnas.1319030111

    A meta-analysis of 225 STEM studies. Active learning raised exam scores, and students in traditional lectures were 1.5 times more likely to fail.

    Used in: 18. Active learning in a large lecture Fall 2027

  16. Funk and Dickson (2011). Crib Card Use During Tests. Teaching of Psychology. doi:10.1177/0098628311401584

    Asks whether crib cards help students or become a crutch they lean on during the test.

    Used in: 43. Exams students can prepare for

  17. Griffin, Cohen, Berndtson, Burson, Camper, Chen and Smith (2014). Starting the Conversation: An Exploratory Study of Factors That Influence Student Office Hour Use. College Teaching. doi:10.1080/87567555.2014.896777

    Why students do and do not come to office hours. It looks at what students think office hours are for and what keeps them away.

    Used in: 34. Alternatives to office hours Spring 2028

  18. Guerrero and Rod (2013). Engaging in Office Hours: A Study of Student-Faculty Interaction and Academic Performance. Journal of Political Science Education. doi:10.1080/15512169.2013.835554

    Students who came to office hours did better in the course than students who did not.

    Used in: 35. Reasons to come to office hours Spring 2028

  19. Haladyna, Downing and Rodriguez (2002). A Review of Multiple-Choice Item-Writing Guidelines for Classroom Assessment. Applied Measurement in Education. doi:10.1207/S15324818AME1503_5

    The standard list of rules for writing multiple-choice questions, drawn from textbooks and research, including writing wrong answers that reflect common errors.

    Used in: 4. Multiple-choice questions that find misconceptions Spring 2027

  20. Harnish and Bridges (2011). Effect of syllabus tone: students' perceptions of instructor and course. Social Psychology of Education. doi:10.1007/s11218-011-9152-4

    The same course described in a warmer syllabus changed how students saw the instructor and the course.

    Used in: 44. A syllabus that runs the semester for you

  21. Hattie and Timperley (2007). The Power of Feedback. Review of Educational Research. doi:10.3102/003465430298487

    Good feedback answers three questions a student has. Where am I going? How am I doing? Where to next? Feedback about the task and how to improve helps most; praise of the person helps least.

    Used in: 1. Rubrics that grade quickly and fairly, 2. Faster feedback with AI drafts and a person in the loop

  22. Johnson and Johnson (2009). An Educational Psychology Success Story: Social Interdependence Theory and Cooperative Learning. Educational Researcher. doi:10.3102/0013189X09339057

    Group work succeeds when members depend on each other to reach the goal and each member is individually accountable for a part.

    Used in: 24. Team projects that are fair to each student Fall 2027, 31. Give each TA a role, not a pile Spring 2028

  23. Jonsson and Svingby (2007). The use of scoring rubrics: Reliability, validity and educational consequences. Educational Research Review. doi:10.1016/j.edurev.2007.05.002

    A review of 75 studies. Rubrics make scoring more consistent when they are analytic, specific to the task, and used with examples and grader training, and they can help students learn by making expectations clear.

    Used in: 1. Rubrics that grade quickly and fairly, 43. Exams students can prepare for

  24. Karpicke and Blunt (2011). Retrieval Practice Produces More Learning than Elaborative Studying with Concept Mapping. Science. doi:10.1126/science.1199327

    Students who practiced recall did better a week later than students who built concept maps with the text open, even on a test that asked for a concept map.

    Used in: 43. Exams students can prepare for

  25. Kazerouni, Edwards and Shaffer (2017). Quantifying Incremental Development Practices and Their Relationship to Procrastination. ACM Conference on International Computing Education Research (ICER). doi:10.1145/3105726.3106180

    Measures, from students' work history, how incrementally they developed their projects and how early they started, and relates those habits to procrastination.

    Used in: 22. GitHub history that shows each student's own work Fall 2027

  26. Kazerouni, Shaffer, Edwards and Servant (2019). Assessing Incremental Testing Practices and Their Impact on Project Outcomes. SIGCSE Technical Symposium. doi:10.1145/3287324.3287366

    Metrics for whether students test as they go, computed from their repositories, and evidence that testing as you go is tied to better project results.

    Used in: 22. GitHub history that shows each student's own work Fall 2027

  27. Krathwohl (2002). A Revision of Bloom's Taxonomy: An Overview. Theory Into Practice. doi:10.1207/s15430421tip4104_2

    The revised Bloom's taxonomy. Outcomes should name what students will be able to do (remember, apply, analyze, evaluate, create), which makes them possible to assess.

    Used in: 15. Course outcomes, each measured two ways Fall 2027

  28. Kuh and Hu (2001). The Effects of Student-Faculty Interaction in the 1990s. The Review of Higher Education. doi:10.1353/rhe.2001.0005

    Contact with faculty outside class, especially about coursework, was linked to students' satisfaction and their reported learning gains.

    Used in: 33. Turning grading hours into student contact Spring 2028

  29. Lim and Guo (2026). Behind the Scenes of Delivering a Large Computing Course: The Experience of a TA Managing Logistics. SIGCSE Technical Symposium. doi:10.1145/3770762.3772547

    A long-time TA's account of running the logistics of a 600-student CS1 course. Much of the work is hidden, planning ahead is what prevents chaos, and overlapping threads of work were the main source of stress.

    Used in: 23. Running a course and its staff with Canvas and GitHub Fall 2027, 30. A 30-minute weekly staff meeting Spring 2028, 38. One answer from every staff member Spring 2028, 41. Keeping staff hours in check Spring 2028

  30. Markel and Guo (2021). Inside the Mind of a CS Undergraduate TA: A Firsthand Account of Undergraduate Peer Tutoring in Computer Labs. SIGCSE Technical Symposium. doi:10.1145/3408877.3432533

    An undergraduate TA's record of 69 help sessions in a CS2 lab, with a model of how tutoring depends on reading the student's thinking and ideas for supporting undergraduate TAs.

    Used in: 37. Undergraduate course assistants: training and support Spring 2028

  31. Messick (1995). Validity of psychological assessment: Validation of inferences from persons' responses and performances as scientific inquiry into score meaning. American Psychologist. doi:10.1037/0003-066X.50.9.741

    The modern view of validity. A score is valid only if it means what we use it to mean, so a grade that depends on who graded it is not valid.

    Used in: 9. Consistent grading across TAs Spring 2027

  32. Meyer, Jansen, Schiller, Liebenow, Steinbach, Horbach and Fleckenstein (2024). Using LLMs to bring evidence-based feedback into the classroom: AI-generated feedback increases secondary students' text revision, motivation, and positive emotions. Computers and Education: Artificial Intelligence. doi:10.1016/j.caeai.2023.100199

    In an experiment with 459 secondary students, AI-generated feedback on essay drafts led to better revisions, more motivation and more positive feelings about the task.

    Used in: 2. Faster feedback with AI drafts and a person in the loop

  33. Nesbit and Adesope (2006). Learning With Concept and Knowledge Maps: A Meta-Analysis. Review of Educational Research. doi:10.3102/00346543076003413

    Across many studies, building or studying concept maps produced more learning than reading text, attending lectures or discussion, with moderate effects.

    Used in: 43. Exams students can prepare for

  34. Nicol (2010). From monologue to dialogue: improving written feedback processes in mass higher education. Assessment & Evaluation in Higher Education. doi:10.1080/02602931003786559

    Written feedback works better as a dialogue than as a one-way message, and the paper shows practical ways to build that dialogue into large courses.

    Used in: 10. Regrade requests without the pain Spring 2027

  35. Nicol, Thomson and Breslin (2014). Rethinking feedback practices in higher education: a peer review perspective. Assessment & Evaluation in Higher Education. doi:10.1080/02602938.2013.795518

    Students learn as much from writing reviews of others' work as from receiving them, because reviewing makes them judge work against a standard.

    Used in: 12. Peer review that helps both sides Spring 2027

  36. Novak (2011). Just-in-time teaching. New Directions for Teaching and Learning. doi:10.1002/tl.469

    Just-in-Time Teaching. Students answer short questions online before class, and the teacher shapes the class around their answers.

    Used in: 7. An FAQ instead of a forum, fed by pre-class checks Spring 2027

  37. Palmer, Bach and Streifer (2014). Measuring the Promise: A Learning-Focused Syllabus Rubric. To Improve the Academy. doi:10.3998/tia.17063888.0033.103

    A rubric for scoring how learning-focused a syllabus is, from its learning goals and assessments to its schedule and tone.

    Used in: 44. A syllabus that runs the semester for you

  38. Panadero and Jonsson (2013). The use of scoring rubrics for formative assessment purposes revisited: A review. Educational Research Review. doi:10.1016/j.edurev.2013.01.002

    Rubrics given to students before the work help them understand the goal, plan, and judge their own work, which supports learning as well as grading.

    Used in: 1. Rubrics that grade quickly and fairly, 43. Exams students can prepare for

  39. Rasooli, Zandi and DeLuca (2019). Conceptualising fairness in classroom assessment: exploring the value of organisational justice theory. Assessment in Education: Principles, Policy & Practice. doi:10.1080/0969594X.2019.1593105

    Fairness in assessment has three parts. Fair outcomes, fair and consistent procedures, and fair treatment in how decisions are explained to students.

    Used in: 39. A log of every student interaction, for fair decisions Spring 2028

  40. Raymark and Connor-Greene (2002). The Syllabus Quiz. Teaching of Psychology. doi:10.1207/s15328023top2904_05

    A short quiz on the syllabus in the first week, as a cheap way to get students to read it.

    Used in: 44. A syllabus that runs the semester for you

  41. Reddy and Andrade (2010). A review of rubric use in higher education. Assessment & Evaluation in Higher Education. doi:10.1080/02602930902862859

    A review of rubrics in college courses. Students value rubrics that are shared in advance, and rubrics can improve both performance and the consistency of grading.

    Used in: 1. Rubrics that grade quickly and fairly

  42. Ren, Krishnamurthi and Fisler (2019). What Help Do Students Seek in TA Office Hours?. ACM Conference on International Computing Education Research (ICER). doi:10.1145/3291279.3339418

    A study of the questions students bring to CS office hours, and what that says about the help TAs give and could give.

    Used in: 36. Office hours that teach, not just fix bugs Spring 2028

  43. Riese, Lorås, Ukrop and Effenberger (2021). Challenges Faced by Teaching Assistants in Computer Science Education Across Europe. ITiCSE. doi:10.1145/3430665.3456304

    A survey of computing TAs at universities across Europe about the challenges of the job, from working with students to working with the course.

    Used in: 29. Onboarding course staff in one week Spring 2028

  44. Roediger and Karpicke (2006). Test-Enhanced Learning. Psychological Science. doi:10.1111/j.1467-9280.2006.01693.x

    Students who practiced recalling a passage remembered much more a week later than students who restudied it, even with no feedback on their answers.

    Used in: 43. Exams students can prepare for

  45. Rohrer and Taylor (2007). The shuffling of mathematics problems improves learning. Instructional Science. doi:10.1007/s11251-007-9015-8

    Mixing problem types in practice, instead of one type at a time, improved later test scores, because students had to choose the method as well as use it.

    Used in: 43. Exams students can prepare for

  46. Ryan, Pintrich and Midgley (2001). Avoiding Seeking Help in the Classroom: Who and Why?. Educational Psychology Review. doi:10.1023/A:1009013420053

    The students who most need help are often the ones who avoid asking, usually because asking feels like a public sign of weakness.

    Used in: 7. An FAQ instead of a forum, fed by pre-class checks Spring 2027

  47. Sadler (1989). Formative assessment and the design of instructional systems. Instructional Science. doi:10.1007/BF00117714

    To improve, students need to know the standard, see how their work compares with it, and know how to close the gap, which means seeing the standard applied to real work.

    Used in: 13. Grading final projects at scale Spring 2027

  48. UMass Amherst Faculty Senate (2024). Required Syllabus Components (Sen. Doc. No. 24-066). UMass Amherst Faculty Senate. www.umass.edu/senate/book/courses-policies-syllabus

    The 14 elements every UMass syllabus must include, from course number to the required statements, and why a clear syllabus is an equity issue.

    Used in: 44. A syllabus that runs the semester for you

  49. Uttl, White and Gonzalez (2017). Meta-analysis of faculty's teaching effectiveness: Student evaluation of teaching ratings and student learning are not related. Studies in Educational Evaluation. doi:10.1016/j.stueduc.2016.08.007

    Across many multisection studies, student ratings of teaching were not related to how much students learned. Read the comments for what to change rather than chasing the score.

    Used in: 14. Reading your course evaluations Spring 2027

  50. Walton and Cohen (2011). A Brief Social-Belonging Intervention Improves Academic and Health Outcomes of Minority Students. Science. doi:10.1126/science.1198364

    A one-hour message that early struggle is normal and passes raised the grades of Black students over three years and halved the achievement gap.

    Used in: 17. The first week: belonging Fall 2027

  51. Wetzel (2025). Transforming Faculty Office Hours: Student-Centered Design Increases Student Perceptions and Usage of Office Hours. College Teaching. doi:10.1080/87567555.2025.2533961

    Office hours redesigned around what students need, rather than the instructor's schedule, were used more and seen more positively by students.

    Used in: 34. Alternatives to office hours Spring 2028

  52. Winkelmes, Bernacki, Butler, Zochowski, Golanics and Weavil (2016). A teaching intervention that increases underserved college students' success. Peer Review (AAC&U). tilthighered.com/

    Transparency in Learning and Teaching (TILT). Spelling out an assignment's purpose, task and criteria improved students' confidence and sense of belonging, with the largest gains for first-generation and underserved students.

    Used in: 1. Rubrics that grade quickly and fairly, 5. Assignment specs students can follow Spring 2027, 16. A syllabus and AI rules students read Fall 2027, 43. Exams students can prepare for, 44. A syllabus that runs the semester for you

  53. Wisniewski, Zierer and Hattie (2020). The Power of Feedback Revisited: A Meta-Analysis of Educational Feedback Research. Frontiers in Psychology. doi:10.3389/fpsyg.2019.03087

    A meta-analysis of 435 studies. Feedback has a medium to large effect on learning overall; detailed feedback on the task and how to improve has a large effect (d = 0.99), rewards and praise alone very little (d = 0.24).

    Used in: 2. Faster feedback with AI drafts and a person in the loop

  54. Woodrow, Malik and Piech (2024). AI Teaches the Art of Elegant Coding: Timely, Fair, and Helpful Style Feedback in a Global Course. SIGCSE Technical Symposium. doi:10.1145/3626252.3630773

    Instant AI feedback on code style for 8,310 students in a free global CS1 course. 97% of raters found it helpful, at a cost of about $90 in all.

    Used in: 2. Faster feedback with AI drafts and a person in the loop

  55. Yang, Luo, Vadillo, Yu and Shanks (2021). Testing (quizzing) boosts classroom learning: A systematic and meta-analytic review. Psychological Bulletin. doi:10.1037/bul0000309

    A meta-analysis of 222 classroom studies. Frequent low-stakes quizzes raised learning by about half a standard deviation (g = 0.50) compared with other ways of studying.

    Used in: 3. Low-stakes quizzes that teach Spring 2027, 43. Exams students can prepare for

  56. Yeager, Purdie-Vaughns, Garcia, Apfel, Brzustoski, Master, Hessert, Williams and Cohen (2014). Breaking the cycle of mistrust: Wise interventions to provide critical feedback across the racial divide. Journal of Experimental Psychology: General. doi:10.1037/a0033906

    Critical feedback with one added line, "I'm giving you these comments because I have high standards and I know you can meet them", led more students to revise, especially students who had reason to mistrust school.

    Used in: 25. Reaching students who fall behind Fall 2027

  57. Zimmerman (2002). Becoming a Self-Regulated Learner: An Overview. Theory Into Practice. doi:10.1207/s15430421tip4102_2

    Self-regulated learners set goals, monitor their own work and adjust, and these are skills that can be taught.

    Used in: 27. Students giving themselves feedback Fall 2027
