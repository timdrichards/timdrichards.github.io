SRTI Analyzer

Student evaluations are biased in known ways. This tool takes your SRTI reports and separates what the numbers can support from what they can't: how far you really are from the department mean, whether the negative comments are more than your grade mix predicts, and which complaints are about you, your course design, or the room.

Runs entirely in your browser. Your PDFs are read on your own computer and never uploaded anywhere. Close the tab and they are gone.

Drop SRTI PDFs here, or

For each course, add both the Section Report and the Open-Ended Responses PDF from OAPA. Add several courses or semesters to see which themes recur.

Assumptions

These stand in for department-wide data the tool does not have yet. Change them and the results update.

How the numbers are made

Gaps from the department

Each item's gap is your mean minus the department mean. Small classes and low response rates make a mean noisy, so the gap is multiplied by a reliability λ = τ² / (τ² + SE²), where SE is the standard error of your mean (with a correction for how much of the class responded) and τ is how much true section means vary. A noisy gap shrinks toward zero.

The grade baseline

Students who expect low grades rate courses lower whatever the teaching. From the expected-grade question, the tool predicts how many students should rate the course 1 or 2, and compares that with how many did. A z near 0 means the low ratings are about what the grades predict. A large positive z means students who were doing fine still rated the course low. Pass/fail courses have no grade spread, so there is no baseline.

Comment themes

Each written response is checked for themes (workload, feedback, difficulty, clarity, and so on). Every theme has a target: you, your course design, the format, the course staff, or things outside your control such as the room or the time slot, which are shown but never scored. Click any theme to see the comments behind it.

Signal

Signal = (shrunk share − baseline) × D × C × R. The share of respondents raising a theme is shrunk toward a baseline so a few comments in a small class don't dominate. D is direction: if as many students say "too easy" as "too hard", the difficulty is probably right (D near 0). C is 1 when a numeric item backs the theme up, 0.5 when not. R is how many of your loaded courses show the theme.

What this can't tell you. SRTIs measure student satisfaction, not learning; large studies find the two barely correlate. If weak teaching causes low grades, the grade baseline excuses it. Comment coding here is a keyword pass and makes mistakes, which is why every theme shows its comments. Use the results to decide what to look at, not as a verdict.
